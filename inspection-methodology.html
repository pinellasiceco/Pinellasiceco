Pinellas Ice Co.
Inspection Estimate Methodology
How Pinellas Ice Co. estimates the likely inspection window
for each business in Pinellas County

What this is: a likely window for when this location may next be inspected — based on its own historical pattern.

What this is not: a guaranteed or exact date. DBPR inspection timing has real, unavoidable variability, and our goal is an honest, useful estimate rather than false precision.

📋
This estimate is specific to your location. It is generated from the DBPR inspection history on file for this business — not a county-wide average or a generic estimate.
1

We start with this specific business's own inspection history

Every Florida food service establishment has a publicly available inspection record maintained by the Division of Hotels and Restaurants (DBPR). That record includes the date of every past inspection, the outcome, and any violations cited.

We analyze this business's own pattern of past visits — how long it was between each inspection, what violations were found, and how the inspector assessed the risk level — to build a model of how this specific location tends to be re-inspected over time.

2

Why that's more useful than a county-wide average

The obvious approach — “Florida restaurants get inspected roughly every four months” — is true in aggregate but nearly useless for any specific location. In practice, inspection cadence varies significantly by risk profile, violation history, and prior outcomes.

Generic estimate
“The average Pinellas restaurant is inspected about every 120 days.”

Applied equally to every business regardless of their actual history.
This forecast
“Based on your past inspection dates and outcomes, your next visit is most likely within this specific window.”

Businesses with a history of ice machine violations are typically re-inspected on a tighter schedule. Businesses with consistently clean records may go longer between visits. Our model accounts for those differences.

3

The estimate is anchored to the most recent inspection on record

The window is projected forward from the most recent confirmed inspection we have on file for this business from the DBPR public records. If a callback or follow-up visit occurred after the original inspection, the estimate is anchored to that more recent date — not the earlier one.

Inspection data is updated regularly from DBPR's public database. If a very recent inspection has not yet propagated through the system, our most recent date on file may lag by a few days.

4

What the predicted interval is based on

The estimate considers several signals from this business's history: the typical gap between its past routine inspections, the kinds of violations found, and how those visits were resolved.

Because cadence shifts as a location's compliance history changes, we weight the pattern of routine inspections to reflect how this specific business tends to be visited over time — rather than applying one fixed interval to everyone.

Important Caveat

This is a prediction based on historical patterns, not a guarantee of when DBPR will actually inspect this location. Inspectors operate on schedules that may be affected by staffing, complaints received, or district priorities that are not reflected in the public record. The predicted date should be read as a likely window, not a certain date.
