What the Citation Actually Means

Important
An ice machine citation means the inspector observed visible contamination, mold, biofilm, or sanitation failure during their visit. Ice is classified as food under Florida law. Contaminated ice is a food safety violation, not a cosmetic issue.
  • ● The citation is on your public record. DBPR inspection reports are published online and searchable by anyone — including customers, journalists, and health advocacy groups.
  • ● A follow-up inspection is coming. DBPR does not treat ice machine citations as self-resolved. An inspector will return to verify the issue has been corrected.
  • ● Repeat violations escalate. A second citation for the same issue can trigger fines, increased inspection frequency, and in serious cases, suspension of your food service permit.
  • ● "We cleaned it ourselves" is not documentation. An inspector can't verify a self-reported cleaning. They need evidence the machine meets food safety standards.

What Happens on the Follow-Up Visit

DBPR follow-up inspections for ice machine violations are focused and specific. The inspector is looking for evidence that the violation was corrected — not a general re-inspection of your establishment.

1
Inspector Returns Unannounced
Follow-up inspections are not scheduled in advance. The inspector may return within days or weeks. You will not receive prior notice.
2
Inspector Examines the Machine
They will open the ice machine and inspect water-contact surfaces, bin interior, water lines, and filters for visible contamination or biofilm. Visual cleanliness is the minimum standard — not the only standard.
3
Inspector Asks for Documentation
They will ask what corrective action was taken and when. A service record showing who cleaned it and when helps. A verified ATP score showing the machine passed biological standards helps more.
4
Violation Is Resolved or Re-Cited
If the machine passes, the violation is marked corrected. If it fails again, you receive a second citation with escalating consequences.
The Documentation Problem
Most professional cleaning services leave a service record with a date. That tells the inspector someone came. It does not tell them whether the machine is actually clean. An ATP bioluminescence test result — showing an RLU score at or below the FDA-acceptable standard of 10 — is objective, measurable evidence that the machine passed biological standards. That's the difference between hoping the inspector agrees it looks clean and proving it with a number.

What Strong Documentation Looks Like

  • ✓ Professional cleaning by a licensed service provider — with a dated service record on company letterhead
  • ✓ ATP bioluminescence test result — showing pre-clean and post-clean RLU scores, with post-clean at or below 10 RLU
  • ✓ Certification on the machine — a cert sticker with a scannable QR code that pulls up the verified test record
  • ✓ A documented service schedule — showing quarterly or semi-annual cleaning as an ongoing practice, not a one-time reaction
  • ✕ Not sufficient: "We cleaned it ourselves." Self-reported cleaning with no third-party verification.
  • ✕ Not sufficient: A receipt from a cleaning service with no biological verification result.
