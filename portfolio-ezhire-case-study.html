Context

A two-sided marketplace in a regulated industry with no existing playbook

EzHire launched in March 2020 into the cannabis industry — a sector with no established recruiting infrastructure, significant regulatory variability by state, and enterprise clients (multi-state operators) who needed a real hiring workflow, not a job board. The product was a full recruitment-as-a-service platform: a custom app with video interviewing, candidate profile management, and employer workflow tools built from scratch.

The challenge
A two-sided marketplace only works when both sides are active. Candidates won't complete profiles if there are no employers. Employers won't subscribe if there are no candidates. The onboarding problem was really two separate problems that had to be solved simultaneously — with no existing user base, no brand recognition, and no template from a comparable industry.

The Critical Discovery

Profile completion had a 24-hour window — after that, the candidate was gone

The problem we found
Candidate drop-off was happening between signup and profile completion
At peak, 500+ candidates were enrolling daily via the automated Indeed pipeline. But a large portion were signing up and not completing their profiles — rendering them invisible to employers and worthless to the marketplace. The data showed a clear pattern: if a candidate didn't complete their profile within the first 24 hours, they almost never came back. Every day of drop-off at that volume was hundreds of incomplete profiles and lost marketplace liquidity.
What we built to fix it: A time-triggered nudge sequence — SMS outreach via Twilio fired within the first few hours of signup, then again before the 24-hour window closed. HubSpot automations tracked completion status in real time and suppressed the sequence once the profile was finished. Incomplete profiles got escalating urgency. Completed profiles got routed immediately into the employer-facing candidate pool. The sequence reduced early drop-off and dramatically improved the ratio of enrolled-to-available candidates in the marketplace.

24-hour SMS completion sequence

Twilio-powered SMS sequence triggered at signup, timed to catch candidates before the 24-hour drop-off window. HubSpot suppression logic stopped messages once the profile was completed — no over-messaging active users.

Employers could subscribe monthly for unlimited candidate access or purchase credits to unlock individual profiles. Both paths were tracked in HubSpot with automated employer-side nurture sequences driving trial-to-paid conversion.

The onboarding insight that mattered most: In a two-sided marketplace, completion rate on the supply side (candidates) directly determines conversion rate on the demand side (employers). Every candidate who dropped off before completing their profile was a piece of inventory that employers couldn't see — which made the platform feel thin and slowed employer conversion. Solving the 24-hour completion problem wasn't a UX fix, it was a business model fix.
