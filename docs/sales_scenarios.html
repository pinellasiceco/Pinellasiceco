The Perfect Close
Gold Lead — Same-Day Close★☆☆☆☆ Easy
▼
Context
LocationWaterfront bar-restaurant, Clearwater Beach
ProspectJake, owner-operator, 12 years at location
TimeWednesday 10:15am — post-breakfast, pre-lunch prep. Low-traffic window.
KitFull kit in vehicle. ATP device in hand.
DBPR — V22 Citation 8 days ago
"Mold observed on interior evaporator components. Corrective action required."
[You walk in. Jake is behind the bar cutting limes. The restaurant is quiet. You approach with the ATP device visible.]
YOU"Hey Jake — I'm [name] with Pinellas Ice Co. We do ATP-certified ice machine cleaning for restaurants on the beach. I pulled your DBPR inspection record this morning — saw you had a citation last week for mold on the evaporator. Mind if I show you something real quick? Takes about 30 seconds."
JAKE"Yeah I saw that. My manager said he cleaned it out. Should be fine now."
YOU"That's great — this will tell us for certain. The ATP device measures biological contamination with a number. If it passes I'll tell you that and we go our separate ways."
[Jake nods. You open the access panel and run the pre-service swab. 15 seconds.]
312 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
JAKE"312. What does that mean exactly?"
YOU"It means there's active biological contamination in contact with your ice right now. Your manager cleaned the accessible surfaces — but the evaporator plates and water distribution lines are where mold establishes, and those need a full descale and sanitize to clear. Can I take a before photo? We document everything."
JAKE"Sure."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
YOU"For $228 today — that's the initial deep clean plus your first month in our quarterly program — we clean the entire machine, run a post-service ATP test to prove it worked, before and after photos on file, certified clean sticker on the machine, and a digital record you can show a health inspector. We come back every 90 days. And if your machine gets cited within 30 days of our service, full refund. We've never had to honor that guarantee."
[Pause. Jake looks at the device screen.]
JAKE"You can do it today?"
YOU"Right now. But if lunch prep is tight, I can also come back between 3 and 5 — before your dinner service."
JAKE"No, do it now. I'll put you in the corner. What do I need to do?"
YOU"Just keep your staff clear of this area while I work. I'll send the payment link — takes 30 seconds."
[You send the Stripe link. Jake pays on his phone. $228 processed. Subscription running. You begin the service.]
[90 minutes later. Post-service swab complete.]
8 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
YOU"You went from 312 to 8. FDA standard is 10. Your machine passes."
[You place the certification sticker on the machine. Hand Jake the printed ATP report.]
YOU"This is your copy. The QR code links to your certification record online. Your staff can scan it, your customers can scan it, and if a health inspector asks about your ice machine you hand them this. We'll be back in 90 days."
JAKE"What was my manager actually cleaning?"
YOU"The bin interior and the external surfaces — what he could reach and see. The evaporator is behind the panel. That's where the biology lives."
JAKE"Good to know. Thank you."
Outcome
✓Closed same day. $228 collected. $129/month subscription active.
✓Certification sticker placed. First ATP report issued.
Key Lesson

The manager's in-house clean failed because surface cleaning never reaches the evaporator. 312 RLU proved it in 15 seconds without an argument. Jake offered the timing hesitation you anticipated — you offered the 3–5pm window which removed it. Your only job was getting permission to run the test. The machine closed the sale. Always offer an alternative time even when same-day is the goal.

The Gatekeeper
Gold Lead — Double Visit Close★★☆☆☆ Moderate
▼
Context
LocationIndependent cafe, downtown Clearwater
ProspectMaria, owner — not present at 10:30am
StaffTyler, server, 20s, not especially helpful
TimeTuesday 10:30am. VM left at 8:15am.
DBPR — V22 Citation 5 days ago
"Slime buildup on ice bin interior and water distribution lines."
[You walk in. Tyler is on his phone behind the counter. Looks up slowly.]
YOU"Hey — is Maria in? I'm [name] with Pinellas Ice Co. I left her a voicemail this morning about her ice machine."
TYLER"She's not in until like 2-ish."
YOU"Got it. Any chance you could make sure she gets this? Tell her I called about the DBPR health inspection — there's something about the ice machine she'll want to see."
[Tyler takes the card without enthusiasm and sets it next to the register. You place a collateral piece face-up beside it.]
YOU"What time does it usually slow down after 2? I don't want to catch her in the middle of the lunch rush."
TYLER"Like 2:30 maybe."
YOU"I'll come back at 2:30. Thanks."
[2:38pm. You return. Maria is behind the counter. She's seen the card but not the voicemail yet — Tyler mentioned someone came by.]
MARIA"You're the ice machine person?"
YOU"That's me. [Name], Pinellas Ice Co. Left you a voicemail this morning — did you get a chance to hear it?"
MARIA"Not yet. What's this about?"
YOU"Five days ago DBPR cited your machine for slime buildup on the ice bin and water lines. I have my ATP device — measures contamination with a number. Takes 30 seconds. Want to see where it reads right now?"
MARIA"I already had someone look at it. My refrigeration company said it was fine."
YOU"The ATP test will confirm that. If it passes I'll tell you that and we go our separate ways. No cost."
[Maria shrugs. You run the pre-service test.]
248 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
MARIA"248. My guy said it was fine."
YOU"He probably cleared the visible buildup — what you can see and reach. The slime in the water distribution lines doesn't come out with surface cleaning. That's what the 248 is."
[Maria crosses her arms. Processing, not hostile.]
MARIA"So what do you do that he doesn't?"
YOU"Full descale on the water system, brush out the evaporator and distribution lines, sanitize all food-contact surfaces, before and after ATP test to prove it worked. Your refrigeration guy is excellent at repairs. This is specifically contamination remediation with documented proof."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
MARIA"What does it cost?"
YOU"$228 today — full deep clean, before and after ATP documentation, certified sticker, and your first month of quarterly service. We come back every 90 days. And if your machine gets cited within 30 days of our service — full refund."
MARIA"I can't do it right now. I'm down a person."
YOU"No problem. I can do early morning before you open — 7:30am — or between lunch and dinner. Thursday work?"
MARIA"Thursday morning. 7:30."
YOU"Done. I'll confirm by text tomorrow night."
[You log Maria in the app. Set a Wednesday evening confirmation text nudge.]
[Thursday 7:35am. You arrive. Maria is prepping pastries. Machine is accessible. Service takes 80 minutes. Post-service swab complete.]
9 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
YOU"You went from 248 to 9. FDA standard is 10. Your machine passes."
[Maria sets down the pastry she was shaping. Looks at both numbers on the report.]
MARIA"248 to 9. That's… that's really different. My guy said it was fine."
YOU"Your refrigeration company did what they were asked to do — they just don't have this tool. They clean what they can see. This gets what they can't reach. Now you have the before and after on record."
[You place the sticker. Hand her the report.]
MARIA"Show me how the QR code works."
[You scan the sticker with your phone. The CleanScore record pulls up — Maria's cafe, service date, 248 pre, 9 post, certification active.]
MARIA"Can my customers actually scan this?"
YOU"Anyone with a phone. Some owners put it where customers can see it. Your call."
MARIA"I like that. Thank you."
Outcome
✓Two visits. Closed Thursday morning. $228 collected. Subscription active.
✓Maria converted from skeptic to advocate. Asked about customer-facing sticker placement.
Key Lesson

Tyler not relaying the message is expected. The VM at 8:15am created context — when you returned at 2:30pm you weren't cold. The refrigeration company objection dissolved when you framed the two services as complementary not competitive. The Thursday 7:30am slot is your best friend — owners are calm, kitchen is empty, machine is accessible. Maria's reaction to seeing 248 become 9 in one service is the emotional payoff of this entire business. Always pause after showing the post-service reading. Let them sit with it.

Commitment Objection
Gold Lead — Flex Plan Close★★★☆☆ Challenging
▼
Context
LocationSports bar, St. Petersburg
ProspectDerek, owner, mid-40s — had a bad experience with vendor lock-in years ago
TimeThursday 11:15am
DBPR — V22 Citation 3 days ago
"Biofilm present on evaporator plate surfaces. Ice bin interior soiled."
[Derek is counting the register. Looks up when you walk in — not unfriendly but busy.]
YOU"Derek — [name] from Pinellas Ice Co. I pulled your DBPR inspection record. You had a citation Tuesday for biofilm on the evaporator. Mind if I show you the actual reading right now? 30 seconds."
DEREK"Sure, why not."
[You run the pre-service test.]
190 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
YOU"190. FDA standard is 10."
DEREK"Okay. What does it cost to fix it?"
YOU"For $228 today — full deep clean, before and after ATP test to prove it worked, certified sticker, and first month of our quarterly program at $129 a month."
DEREK"Yeah I'm not doing a monthly thing. I hate subscriptions. Just do the cleaning, what does that cost?"
YOU"What specifically concerns you about the monthly piece?"
DEREK"Last vendor I had a contract with, I was stuck for two years. Couldn't cancel, price went up, never saw a technician. I'm not doing that again."
YOU"That's fair. We don't have a contract. Cancel anytime with 30 days notice — no annual commitment, no early termination fee. If you want to stop, you let me know 30 days out and you're done. That's it."
DEREK"Seriously? No contract?"
YOU"None. The only thing I ask for is 30 days notice so I can adjust the route. The $228 today covers the clean and your first month. After that it's $129 a month and we're back every 90 days."
[Pause. Derek looks at the device.]
DEREK"And if I'm not happy with the service?"
YOU"If your machine gets cited by a health inspector within 30 days of our service, full refund. And if you're ever not happy with a visit, call me directly. I'm not a call center."
DEREK"What's that sticker thing you mentioned?"
YOU"Goes on the machine. QR code on it — anyone can scan it and see your certification record. Before and after readings, service date, next service due. Some owners like having it visible for staff accountability. A health inspector sees it and knows the machine has a documented service history."
[Derek is quiet for a moment. Looks at the 190 on the device screen.]
DEREK"Alright. No contract, cancel anytime, money back if I get cited. Let's do it."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
YOU"I'll send you the payment link — 30 seconds."
Outcome
✓Closed with flex plan framing. $228 collected. No annual commitment. Subscription active.
Key Lesson

Derek's objection had nothing to do with price or value — it was a past trauma with a vendor. Asking "what specifically concerns you" before offering a solution is critical. If you'd gone straight to the flex plan before understanding his real objection, it might not have landed. The guarantee question — "what if I'm not happy?" — is the deeper version of the commitment objection. Answer it directly: your number, not a call center. That's the real differentiator for someone who's been burned before.

I Already Have Someone
Competitor Displacement★★★★☆ Hard
▼
Context
LocationFamily restaurant, Largo
ProspectCarol, owner — has used Polar Ice Machines for 4+ years. Loyal to Tara personally.
TimeFriday 10:45am
NotePolar is the incumbent. Biannual service, no ATP testing. Carol trusts Tara.
DBPR — V22 Citation 11 days ago
"Mold observed on interior surfaces and evaporator components."
[Carol is talking to a vendor rep when you walk in. She finishes and turns to you with a polite but closed expression.]
YOU"Carol — [name] with Pinellas Ice Co. Do you have 30 seconds? I pulled your DBPR record and wanted to show you something about your ice machine."
CAROL"We already have someone for that. Polar Ice Machines — Tara's been taking care of us for years. She's great."
YOU"That's good to hear. Do you know what your machine scored on the last ATP test Tara ran?"
CAROL"The what?"
YOU"ATP test. It measures biological contamination with a number — FDA standard for food-contact surfaces is 10. It's how you verify the cleaning actually worked."
CAROL"I don't think they do that. Tara just cleans it."
YOU"Most companies don't. The cleaning itself is good practice — the measurement is how you prove it worked. Would you mind if I tested yours right now? If it reads clean I'll tell you, and you can feel good about what Tara's been doing. No cost, 30 seconds."
[Carol hesitates, then:]
CAROL"Fine. But Tara was just here a few months ago."
[You run the pre-service test.]
156 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
CAROL"Tara cleaned this a few months ago. She's always done right by us."
YOU"I'm sure she has — she's clearly been maintaining the relationship. The 156 tells us the evaporator and water lines still have active contamination after the last service. That's not about Tara personally — biannual cleaning without ATP verification means there's no way to know if the hard-to-reach components were cleared. This is a methodology issue, not a quality issue."
CAROL"So you're saying she's not doing her job?"
YOU"I'm saying her method doesn't include a measurement step. Without ATP testing after service there's no way for her — or for you — to know if it worked. Your DBPR citation 11 days ago documented mold that was there after Tara's last visit."
[Silence. Carol looks at the device.]
CAROL"What would you charge?"
YOU"$228 today — full deep clean, before and after ATP test to prove it worked, certified sticker, documented report. First month of quarterly service included. If your machine gets cited within 30 days, full refund."
CAROL"I'm not ready to drop Polar today. Tara's been good to us."
YOU"You don't have to. Let me do this one service. You'll see the before and after numbers. If you're not convinced it's worth continuing, we're done. The $228 stands on its own — no pressure on what comes after."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
CAROL"Okay. Do it."
[Service completed. 85 minutes.]
7 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
YOU"156 to 7. Your machine passes. Here's your report."
[Carol looks at both numbers on the report.]
CAROL"Why couldn't Tara get it to that?"
YOU"Her process probably doesn't include the evaporator descale or the water line flush. Standard cleaning gets the surfaces — the chemistry and the brushwork get what's behind the panel. Different methodology."
CAROL"I need to think about what to do about Tara."
YOU"That's completely your call. Your subscription is running — we'll be back in 90 days. In the meantime, that sticker and QR code are your documentation if a health inspector asks."
[Day 7. Nudge fires in app. You text Carol:]
YOU (text)"Hey Carol — [name] from Pinellas Ice. Just checking in. Hope the report was useful. Any questions about the certification or the next service?"
CAROL (text)"I've been thinking about it. I told Tara about the ATP test. She said she'd look into it. For now I'm going to keep your subscription running and see how the next inspection goes."
YOU (text)"That's the right call. Next service in 83 days. You're certified and documented. Good luck with the inspector."
Outcome
✓Decoupled first close — $228 standalone service. Day 7 nudge confirmed subscription active.
✓Carol keeping both vendors short-term. Your certification is now the standard she'll compare Tara against.
Key Lesson

Never attack the incumbent by name. You didn't say Tara was bad — you said her method has no measurement step, which is factually true. Carol defending Tara was expected — 4 years of loyalty doesn't dissolve in one conversation. The decoupled offer removed the switching barrier. 156 to 7 made your case better than anything you said. Carol's Day 7 response — keeping both vendors — is actually a win. When the next inspection comes and the inspector sees the CleanScore sticker, the outcome is determined.

Corporate Account
Corporate — Multi-Step Close★★★★★ Expert
▼
Context
LocationClearwater Beach hotel bar
ProspectMarcus, F&B Manager — reports to regional director, cannot approve vendor spend
TimeMonday 11am
NoteAny spend over $150 requires regional director approval. Decision is not today.
DBPR — V22 Citation 6 days ago
"Biofilm and scale buildup on evaporator plate surfaces."
[Marcus is setting up the bar. Professional, organized, clearly runs a tight operation.]
YOU"Marcus — [name] with Pinellas Ice Co. Pulled your DBPR record — saw you had a citation last week for biofilm on the evaporator. Mind if I test it? 30 seconds."
MARCUS"Yeah, the GM mentioned it. Go ahead."
[You run the pre-service test.]
287 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
MARCUS"That's not good."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
YOU"287 against a standard of 10. For $228 today we deep clean it, prove it with a post-service ATP, get you a certified sticker and quarterly service at $129 a month."
MARCUS"I can't authorize that. Any vendor spend over $150 goes through my regional director. I'd need to submit a request."
YOU"Completely understand. How long does that process usually take?"
MARCUS"Could be a week, could be three. Depends on what's in the queue."
YOU"Here's what I'd suggest."
[You print the ATP report from your phone — showing 287 RLU, FDA standard reference, company name and certification info.]
YOU"This is a documented pre-service reading from today. When you submit the vendor request, attach this. It shows the machine is reading 287 against an FDA standard of 10, and there's an active V22 citation from last week on record. That's your business case for approval — active compliance issue, documented evidence, specific vendor solution."
MARCUS"That actually helps a lot. My director responds faster when there's compliance documentation rather than just a vendor pitch."
MARCUS"What does the ongoing monthly look like? My director will ask."
YOU"$129 a month after the initial service — quarterly visits, ATP-verified each time. For a property this size it's probably worth including in the request."
MARCUS"I'll put it in the notes."
YOU"One more thing — you can verify the inspection record independently. Go to the DBPR website, search this property name. Everything I showed you today is on the public record. If your director has questions about the source, that's where to point them."
MARCUS"That's useful. I'll submit this afternoon."
YOU"I'm in this area every Tuesday and Thursday. When you get approval, call me and I can usually be here same day. The machine is at 287 — the sooner we address it before your next inspection the better."
[You log Marcus in the app as Quoted stage. Set a 7-day follow-up nudge.]
[Day 9. Nudge fires. You text Marcus:]
YOU (text)"Hey Marcus — [name] from Pinellas Ice. Following up on the vendor request. Any update from your director? Happy to provide any additional documentation if helpful."
MARCUS (text)"Approved this morning. Can you do Thursday at 2pm?"
YOU (text)"Thursday 2pm. Confirmed."
Outcome
✓No same-day close. Day 9 follow-up via nudge system. Closed Thursday.
✓$228 collected. Corporate account established. Hotel has 3 machines — potential expansion.
Key Lesson

Marcus wasn't saying no — he was saying not without approval. Your job shifted from closing to enabling. The printed ATP report became his business case document. The DBPR self-lookup gave him independent verification for his director. The nudge system did the follow-up without you carrying it in your head. Corporate accounts take longer but close cleanly once approved and rarely churn — compliance anxiety is institutional, not personal. Always ask about the approval process early in the conversation.

The Brush-Off
Gold Lead — Partial Win★★☆☆☆ Moderate
▼
Context
LocationBar-restaurant, Largo
ProspectRay, owner, mid-50s
TimeFriday 11:35am — worst possible timing. Kitchen prep in full swing. Ray is moving fast.
NoteYou will face this interaction constantly. It is not a failure. It is a seed.
DBPR — V22 Citation 14 days ago
"Mold observed on evaporator components."
[You walk in. Ray is moving between the kitchen pass and the bar. Glances at you — no welcome, no hostility, just busy.]
YOU"Ray — [name] with Pinellas Ice Co. I pulled your DBPR record. You had a mold citation on your ice machine two weeks ago. Mind if I—"
RAY"We're all set, thanks."
[He turns back to his work. This is the brush-off. You have one line. Use it.]
YOU"Totally understand. I pulled your citation before I came in — V22, mold on the evaporator, two weeks ago. If you ever want to know what it's actually reading before the inspector comes back, I'm in Largo every Tuesday. Here's my card."
[You place the card and a collateral piece on the bar within Ray's sightline. Not forced into his hand — visible and accessible.]
RAY[Without looking up] "Leave it there."
YOU"Will do. Come back Tuesday if the timing's better."
[You walk out. Log Ray in the app. Note: brush-off, card left, DBPR citation mentioned. Set a 7-day nudge.]
[Tuesday following week. 10:15am. You return. Ray is doing paperwork at the bar. Quiet.]
RAY[Recognizes you] "The ice machine guy."
YOU"That's me. Better timing?"
RAY"Yeah, what's this about?"
YOU"Your machine had a mold citation two weeks ago. I have my ATP device — measures contamination with a number. Takes 30 seconds. Want to see where it reads?"
[Ray pauses the paperwork.]
RAY"Alright. Show me."
[You run the pre-service test.]
224 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
RAY"224. That's bad?"
YOU"22 times the FDA limit. The mold from your citation is still active."
RAY"Okay. What do you charge?"
Outcome
✓First visit: brush-off. Card left. Seed planted.
✓Second visit (Tuesday): ATP test run. Pitch continues from here into standard close sequence.
Key Lesson

The brush-off is your most common interaction. It is not a lost prospect — it is a delayed one. One more line after the first no. No second line. No argument. No desperation. You have one line and you use it to plant a specific seed: the DBPR citation, your awareness of it, and a specific day you'll be back. Friday at 11:30am is bad timing — you already knew that. But you walked in because it was on the route. The card and the collateral stayed. The Tuesday return was warm. The timing of your walk-ins matters — 10am–11:30am and 2pm–4pm are your windows. Avoid the 11:30am–1:30pm lunch crush whenever possible.

Machine Reads Clean
Non-Cited — Honest Conversation★★★☆☆ Challenging
▼
Context
LocationJuice bar/cafe, St. Petersburg
ProspectLisa, owner, detail-oriented — cleans the machine herself every month
TimeTuesday 2:15pm
NoteNo ice machine citation on record. You stopped in speculatively after a nearby gold lead.
[Lisa is wiping down the counter. Clean, well-run space. You notice the ice machine — newer model, well-positioned.]
YOU"Hi — I'm [name] with Pinellas Ice Co. We do ATP-certified ice machine testing for restaurants in St. Pete. Mind if I test yours real quick? Completely free — just curious where it reads."
LISA"Sure, go ahead. I clean it myself every month so I'm not worried."
[You run the pre-service test.]
8 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
YOU"Your machine reads 8. FDA standard is 10. You pass."
LISA"I told you."
YOU"You did. And I mean this genuinely — most machines we test in Pinellas read between 150 and 400 before service. Yours is exceptional. What are you using to clean it?"
LISA"Just the manufacturer cleaner. I do it the first of every month."
YOU"Monthly is much more frequent than most operators and you're doing it consistently. That's why it reads 8."
LISA"So you don't need to sell me anything."
YOU"No. But let me ask you something — do you have any documentation of these readings? If a health inspector asks about your ice machine, you can say you clean it monthly, but you can't show a verified number."
LISA"I hadn't thought about that."
YOU"That's what we provide. For $99 — first certified clean with before and after ATP documentation — you get an official report with your readings and a certification sticker with a QR code. Reads 8 today. After our service it might read 4 or 5. Either way you have a verified record."
LISA"The sticker goes on the machine?"
YOU"Right here on the front. Customers can scan it. Inspectors can scan it. Shows your certification history."
LISA"Does it make me look like I had a problem?"
YOU"It makes you look like you take food safety seriously enough to have it professionally verified. It's the same reason restaurants post their A grade from the health department."
LISA"That's a good point. What's the ongoing cost?"
YOU"$129 a month, quarterly service. Given your machine reads 8 right now, you'd likely pass every visit — you'd just have the documentation."
LISA"Let me think about it. But honestly, the sticker idea is interesting."
YOU"Here's my card. The $99 first service stands on its own if you want the documentation without committing to quarterly. Take your time."
[Log Lisa in app. Set 7-day nudge. Note: machine reads 8, owner cleans monthly, interested in certification/documentation angle.]
Outcome
✓No close today. Machine is legitimately clean.
✓Warm prospect — interested in certification angle. 7-day nudge set. Potential referral source.
Key Lesson

A clean machine is not a lost sale — it is trust currency. You told Lisa her machine was clean when you could have said anything. She knows that now. The certification angle is the real pitch for clean-machine prospects: they already do the work, they just don't have the verified record. The sticker on a clean machine is actually more valuable than a sticker on a cleaned machine — it shows the operation was clean to begin with. Lisa is also your best referral source. She'll tell other restaurant owners in the neighborhood she got tested and passed. Some of those neighbors won't pass.

Timing and Access
Gold Lead — Timing Negotiation★★★☆☆ Challenging
▼
Context
LocationBusy lunch restaurant, Clearwater
ProspectSandra, GM — owner is not local, Sandra runs operations
TimeWednesday 12:15pm — middle of lunch rush. Machine is in the kitchen, requires kitchen downtime.
DBPR — V22 Citation 9 days ago
"Significant mold and biofilm on evaporator plate surfaces."
[You walk in. Dining room is 80% full. Sandra is managing the floor — clearly competent, clearly busy. She sees you and comes over before you can speak.]
SANDRA"If this is a sales call it's really bad timing."
YOU"I know — I'll be 60 seconds. I pulled your DBPR record. You had a significant mold citation nine days ago on your ice machine. I have my ATP device. I can't test it right now — I get that — but I wanted to make sure you knew I'd been here. When is the machine accessible?"
SANDRA"The kitchen doesn't clear out until around 3. Then we start dinner prep at 4:30."
YOU"3 to 4:30 is a perfect window. Can I come back today at 3?"
SANDRA"Today's tight. Tomorrow?"
YOU"Tomorrow 3pm works. I'll be here."
SANDRA"What exactly are you testing for?"
YOU"ATP contamination. Gives a numerical reading — FDA standard for ice machine surfaces is 10. Most machines with an active mold citation read between 150 and 400. Takes 15 seconds. If it passes I'll tell you."
SANDRA"And if it doesn't?"
YOU"Then we talk about fixing it. But let me test it first — no commitment."
SANDRA"Fine. 3pm tomorrow."
[You hand her a card and the collateral piece. Log appointment in app. Set confirmation nudge for tomorrow morning.]
[Thursday 3:05pm. Kitchen is quiet. You run the pre-service test.]
318 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
SANDRA"318. That's not good."
YOU"No. But we can fix it today if you have 90 minutes. The window is perfect."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
YOU"For $228 — full deep clean, before and after ATP proof, certification sticker, and first month quarterly. If your machine gets cited within 30 days, full refund."
SANDRA"I need to loop in the owner on any vendor contracts."
YOU"This doesn't have to be a contract. Cancel anytime with 30 days notice. The $228 today covers the clean. If the owner wants to discuss the ongoing program after you show him the before and after results, I'm easy to reach."
SANDRA"Okay. Let's do the clean. I'll send the payment."
Outcome
✓Wrong timing on first visit — correct response was to schedule immediately, not pitch.
✓Second visit (Thursday 3pm): 318 RLU, same-day close. $228 collected. Subscription pending owner review.
Key Lesson

Walking in during a lunch rush is a timing mistake you'll sometimes make. The correct response is not to apologize and leave — it's to acknowledge the timing and immediately own the scheduling. "I know this is bad timing — when is the machine accessible?" converts a bad-timing walk-in into a booked appointment. Sandra's "today's tight" was about logistics, not the service. Once access was scheduled, the close was standard. Always ask about access timing before assuming you can run the test on arrival.

Multi-Machine Upsell
Multi-Location Upsell★★★☆☆ Challenging
▼
