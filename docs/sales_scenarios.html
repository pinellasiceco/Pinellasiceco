The Perfect Close
Gold Lead — Same-Day Close★☆☆☆☆ Easy
▼
Context
LocationWaterfront bar-restaurant, Clearwater Beach
ProspectJake, owner-operator, 12 years at location
TimeWednesday 10:15am — post-breakfast, pre-lunch prep. Low-traffic window.
KitFull kit in vehicle. ATP device in hand.
DBPR — V22 Citation 8 days ago
"Mold observed on interior evaporator components. Corrective action required."
[You walk in. Jake is behind the bar cutting limes. The restaurant is quiet. You approach with the ATP device visible.]
YOU"Hey Jake — I'm [name] with Pinellas Ice Co. We do ATP-certified ice machine cleaning for restaurants on the beach. I pulled your DBPR inspection record this morning — saw you had a citation last week for mold on the evaporator. Mind if I show you something real quick? Takes about 30 seconds."
JAKE"Yeah I saw that. My manager said he cleaned it out. Should be fine now."
YOU"That's great — this will tell us for certain. The ATP device measures biological contamination with a number. If it passes I'll tell you that and we go our separate ways."
[Jake nods. You open the access panel and run the pre-service swab. 15 seconds.]
312 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
JAKE"312. What does that mean exactly?"
YOU"It means there's active biological contamination in contact with your ice right now. Your manager cleaned the accessible surfaces — but the evaporator plates and water distribution lines are where mold establishes, and those need a full descale and sanitize to clear. Can I take a before photo? We document everything."
JAKE"Sure."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
YOU"For $228 today — that's the initial deep clean plus your first month in our quarterly program — we clean the entire machine, run a post-service ATP test to prove it worked, before and after photos on file, certified clean sticker on the machine, and a digital record you can show a health inspector. We come back every 90 days. And if your machine gets cited within 30 days of our service, full refund. We've never had to honor that guarantee."
[Pause. Jake looks at the device screen.]
JAKE"You can do it today?"
YOU"Right now. But if lunch prep is tight, I can also come back between 3 and 5 — before your dinner service."
JAKE"No, do it now. I'll put you in the corner. What do I need to do?"
YOU"Just keep your staff clear of this area while I work. I'll send the payment link — takes 30 seconds."
[You send the Stripe link. Jake pays on his phone. $228 processed. Subscription running. You begin the service.]
[90 minutes later. Post-service swab complete.]
8 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
YOU"You went from 312 to 8. FDA standard is 10. Your machine passes."
[You place the certification sticker on the machine. Hand Jake the printed ATP report.]
YOU"This is your copy. The QR code links to your certification record online. Your staff can scan it, your customers can scan it, and if a health inspector asks about your ice machine you hand them this. We'll be back in 90 days."
JAKE"What was my manager actually cleaning?"
YOU"The bin interior and the external surfaces — what he could reach and see. The evaporator is behind the panel. That's where the biology lives."
JAKE"Good to know. Thank you."
Outcome
✓Closed same day. $228 collected. $129/month subscription active.
✓Certification sticker placed. First ATP report issued.
Key Lesson

The manager's in-house clean failed because surface cleaning never reaches the evaporator. 312 RLU proved it in 15 seconds without an argument. Jake offered the timing hesitation you anticipated — you offered the 3–5pm window which removed it. Your only job was getting permission to run the test. The machine closed the sale. Always offer an alternative time even when same-day is the goal.

Context
LocationIndependent cafe, downtown Clearwater
ProspectMaria, owner — not present at 10:30am
StaffTyler, server, 20s, not especially helpful
TimeTuesday 10:30am. VM left at 8:15am.
DBPR — V22 Citation 5 days ago
"Slime buildup on ice bin interior and water distribution lines."

TYLER"She's not in until like 2-ish."
YOU"Got it. Any chance you could make sure she gets this? Tell her I called about the DBPR health inspection — there's something about the ice machine she'll want to see."

MARIA"Not yet. What's this about?"
YOU"Five days ago DBPR cited your machine for slime buildup on the ice bin and water lines. I have my ATP device — measures contamination with a number. Takes 30 seconds. Want to see where it reads right now?"
MARIA"I already had someone look at it. My refrigeration company said it was fine."
YOU"The ATP test will confirm that. If it passes I'll tell you that and we go our separate ways. No cost."

248 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
MARIA"248. My guy said it was fine."
YOU"He probably cleared the visible buildup — what you can see and reach. The slime in the water distribution lines doesn't come out with surface cleaning. That's what the 248 is."
[Maria crosses her arms. Processing, not hostile.]
MARIA"So what do you do that he doesn't?"
YOU"Full descale on the water system, brush out the evaporator and distribution lines, sanitize all food-contact surfaces, before and after ATP test to prove it worked. Your refrigeration guy is excellent at repairs. This is specifically contamination remediation with documented proof."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
MARIA"What does it cost?"
YOU"$228 today — full deep clean, before and after ATP documentation, certified sticker, and your first month of quarterly service. We come back every 90 days. And if your machine gets cited within 30 days of our service — full refund."
MARIA"I can't do it right now. I'm down a person."
YOU"No problem. I can do early morning before you open — 7:30am — or between lunch and dinner. Thursday work?"
MARIA"Thursday morning. 7:30."
YOU"Done. I'll confirm by text tomorrow night."
[You log Maria in the app. Set a Wednesday evening confirmation text nudge.]
[Thursday 7:35am. You arrive. Maria is prepping pastries. Machine is accessible. Service takes 80 minutes.]
[Post-service swab complete.]
9 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
YOU"You went from 248 to 9. FDA standard is 10. Your machine passes."
[Maria stops what she's doing. Looks at the two numbers.]
[Maria sets down the pastry she was shaping. Looks at both numbers on the report.]
MARIA"248 to 9. That's… that's really different. My guy said it was fine."
YOU"Your refrigeration company did what they were asked to do — they just don't have this tool. They clean what they can see. This gets what they can't reach. Now you have the before and after on record."
[You place the sticker. Hand her the report.]
MARIA"Show me how the QR code works."
[You scan the sticker with your phone. The CleanScore record pulls up — Maria's cafe, service date, 248 pre, 9 post, certification active.]
MARIA"Can my customers actually scan this?"
YOU"Anyone with a phone. Some owners put it where customers can see it. Your call."
MARIA"I like that. Thank you."
Outcome
✓Two visits. Closed Thursday morning. $228 collected. Subscription active.
✓Maria converted from skeptic to advocate. Asked about customer-facing sticker placement.
Key Lesson

Tyler not relaying the message is expected. The VM at 8:15am created context — when you returned at 2:30pm you weren't cold. The refrigeration company objection dissolved when you framed the two services as complementary not competitive. The Thursday 7:30am slot is your best friend — owners are calm, kitchen is empty, machine is accessible. Maria's reaction to seeing 248 become 9 in one service is the emotional payoff of this entire business. Always pause after showing the post-service reading. Let them sit with it.

Commitment Objection
Gold Lead — Flex Plan Close★★★☆☆ Challenging
▼
Context
LocationSports bar, St. Petersburg
ProspectDerek, owner, mid-40s — had a bad experience with vendor lock-in years ago
TimeThursday 11:15am
DBPR — V22 Citation 3 days ago
"Biofilm present on evaporator plate surfaces. Ice bin interior soiled."
[Derek is counting the register. Looks up when you walk in — not unfriendly but busy.]
YOU"Derek — [name] from Pinellas Ice Co. I pulled your DBPR inspection record. You had a citation Tuesday for biofilm on the evaporator. Mind if I show you the actual reading right now? 30 seconds."
DEREK"Sure, why not."
[You run the pre-service test.]
190 RLU
FDA Standard: 10 RLU
YOU"190. FDA standard is 10."
DEREK"Okay. What does it cost to fix it?"
YOU"For $228 today — full deep clean, before and after ATP test to prove it worked, certified sticker, and first month of our quarterly program at $129 a month."
DEREK"Yeah I'm not doing a monthly thing. I hate subscriptions. Just do the cleaning, what does that cost?"
YOU"What specifically concerns you about the monthly piece?"
DEREK"Last vendor I had a contract with, I was stuck for two years. Couldn't cancel, price went up, never saw a technician. I'm not doing that again."
YOU"That's fair. We don't have a contract. Cancel anytime with 30 days notice — no annual commitment, no early termination fee. If you want to stop, you let me know 30 days out and you're done. That's it."
DEREK"Seriously? No contract?"
YOU"None. The only thing I ask for is 30 days notice so I can adjust the route. The $228 today covers the clean and your first month. After that it's $129 a month and we're back every 90 days."
[Pause. Derek looks at the device.]
DEREK"And if I'm not happy with the service?"
YOU"If your machine gets cited by a health inspector within 30 days of our service, full refund. And if you're ever not happy with a visit, call me directly. I'm not a call center."
DEREK"What's that sticker thing you mentioned?"
YOU"Goes on the machine. QR code on it — anyone can scan it and see your certification record. Before and after readings, service date, next service due. Some owners like having it visible for staff accountability. A health inspector sees it and knows the machine has a documented service history."
[Derek is quiet for a moment. Looks at the 190 on the device screen.]
DEREK"Alright. No contract, cancel anytime, money back if I get cited. Let's do it."
[You photograph the interior — document the pre-service condition before touching anything.]
YOU"I'll send you the payment link — 30 seconds."
Outcome
✓Closed with flex plan framing. $228 collected. No annual commitment. Subscription active.
Key Lesson

Derek's objection had nothing to do with price or value — it was a past trauma with a vendor. Asking "what specifically concerns you" before offering a solution is critical. If you'd gone straight to the flex plan before understanding his real objection, it might not have landed. The guarantee question — "what if I'm not happy?" — is the deeper version of the commitment objection. Answer it directly: your number, not a call center. That's the real differentiator for someone who's been burned before.

I Already Have Someone
The Existing Vendor Objection
Warm Convert
Setting: Carol, owner of a family restaurant in Largo. Well-run place, attentive. You walk in during a slow Tuesday afternoon. She's skeptical but polite — she's been using the same HVAC company for "ice maintenance" for two years.
You:
Hi Carol — I'm [Name] with Pinellas Ice Co. Quick question before I let you go: do you know your ice machine's ATP reading right now?
Carol:
We already have someone who handles that. Our HVAC company comes out twice a year.
You:
That's great — most HVAC companies do a solid job on filters and coils. Do they do ATP testing? Like an actual swab that reads contamination in real time?
Carol:
I'm... not sure. They do a cleaning. I assume they check it.
You:
Totally fair. Here's the honest truth — most HVAC visits are visual inspections and filter swaps. ATP testing is a completely different thing. It's a bioluminescence swab that reads bacterial contamination on contact surfaces inside the machine. A lot of cleaned-looking machines still fail because the biofilm is invisible. Can I show you right now? Takes two minutes, costs you nothing, and if your machine passes I'll shake your hand and leave.
Carol:
Fine. Sure, go ahead.
ATP RESULT — Carol's Machine
156 RLU
Before — Ice chute contact surface
FAIL — Threshold is <10 RLU. Active contamination present despite recent HVAC visit.
You:
So — 156. Anything above 10 is a fail. Above 100 is what health inspectors classify as high-risk. I'm not saying your HVAC guy did a bad job on the refrigeration side. But ATP testing isn't part of standard HVAC service. It's a specialty. That's what we do.
Carol:
I just signed a service contract with them last month.
You:
You don't have to cancel anything. What we do is different — we're not replacing your HVAC service. We're the ice-specific certification layer. A lot of our clients keep their existing vendor for refrigeration and add us for ATP compliance and documentation. Two different scopes.
Carol:
What does that cost?
You:
The first visit — full ATP-guided deep clean, before and after readings, CleanScore certification — is $228 today. That covers the initial service and your first month of the quarterly program. After that it's $129 a month, four times a year. You get a CleanScore sticker, a compliance certificate, and documented proof that your machine is clean. If you ever get cited within 30 days of a service, full refund.
Carol:
Let me think about it.
You:
Of course. But I'll leave you with this: your machine is sitting at 156 right now. If a health inspector walks in this afternoon, that's a citation. I can clean it and retest in about 45 minutes. The risk of waiting is real — and it's documented. You want me to go ahead today?
Carol:
Okay. Do it.
ATP RESULT — After Service
7 RLU
After — Same contact surface
PASS — 156 → 7. 96% contamination reduction. CleanScore active.
Outcome
Same-day close at $228. Carol keeps her HVAC vendor. Pinellas Ice Co enrolled as her ATP compliance layer. She texts you the next month: "DBPR inspector came Tuesday. Didn't touch the ice machine."
Key Lesson: Never argue with an existing vendor. Reframe the scope — you're not competing, you're complementary. ATP testing is a specialty the HVAC company isn't doing. The number (156) does the arguing for you.

287 when I swabbed the ice chute last Tuesday. Anything above 10 is a fail. Above 100 is high-risk territory. I'd expect similar readings across your properties if they're on the same cleaning schedule.

Not a good time. Come back another day.

Totally — I'll be quick. We do contamination testing on ice machines, takes two minutes, tells you if your machine would pass a health inspection right now. I'll leave my card. When's a better time — do you have 10 minutes Tuesday morning before open?
Ray:
I don't know. Maybe. Just leave your card.
You:
You got it. I'll write Tuesday 10am on the back — if that works, just text me. If not, we'll find another time. [Name], Pinellas Ice Co.
Tuesday — Return Visit (you show up at 10am as suggested):
You:
Ray — [Name], I left my card Thursday. Said I'd come by Tuesday. Got two minutes?

Yeah, sure. What is this exactly?
You:
We test ice machines for contamination using an ATP luminometer — same thing hospitals use for surface testing. Takes 90 seconds to swab the inside of your machine. You get a real number: pass is under 10, fail is anything above that. I do it right now, no charge, and you see exactly where you stand.
Ray:
And if it fails?
You:
Then I tell you what I found, what it costs to fix it, and you decide. No pressure. But you'll know — which is more than most bar owners can say about their machine.
Ray:
All right. Go ahead.
ATP RESULT — Ray's Machine
203 RLU
Ice chute interior — before service
FAIL — 203 RLU. Biofilm likely present. High-risk for health citation.
You:
203. That's in the high-risk range. Anything over 100 and a health inspector's going to flag it. I can do the full service right now — ATP-guided deep clean, retest to confirm, CleanScore certificate. It's $228 today, covers the service and starts your quarterly program.
Ray:
Let's do it.
ATP RESULT — After Service
6 RLU
Same surface — after deep clean
PASS — 203 → 6. Clean. CleanScore sticker issued.
Outcome
Two-visit close. First visit planted the seed; Tuesday return converted. The key was naming a specific time ("Tuesday 10am") instead of a vague "I'll be back." Ray is now a quarterly client.
Key Lesson: A brush-off isn't a no — it's a timing problem. Never leave without naming a specific return time. Writing it on the back of your card creates a micro-commitment. Show up exactly when you said you would. That reliability closes the door before the ATP test even starts.

Clean Machine Objection
"We Just Had It Cleaned Last Month"
Data Wins
Setting: Sandra, manager of a St. Pete waterfront seafood restaurant. Confident, organized. The machine was cleaned by a national service company 4 weeks ago. She has the receipt.
You:
Hi Sandra — I'm [Name] with Pinellas Ice Co. Quick question: when was your ice machine last serviced?
Sandra:
Actually, we just had it done last month. National company. Full cleaning.
You:
That's great — good that you're on top of it. Can I ask: did they do ATP testing? An actual swab reading that gives you a number?
Sandra:
I don't think so. They cleaned it and certified it. I have the invoice.
You:
I believe you — and the cleaning probably helped. But here's the thing: visual certification and ATP certification are two completely different standards. ATP testing measures live contamination at the cellular level. A machine can look clean and still fail. I'd love to just show you where yours sits right now. If it passes, great — you've got proof your vendor is doing their job. If it doesn't, you'll want to know before an inspector does. Two minutes, no charge.
Sandra:
Okay. I'm curious now.
ATP RESULT — Sandra's Machine
89 RLU
Ice chute — 4 weeks post-service
FAIL — 89 RLU. Below high-risk threshold but active contamination. The prior service missed biofilm.
You:
So — 89. Pass is under 10. This isn't dire, but it's a fail. The prior service cleaned the accessible surfaces but didn't eliminate the biofilm. That's common — biofilm clings to corners and hard-to-reach spots. ATP-guided cleaning means we swab as we go, so we know exactly what we've eliminated and what we haven't. We don't stop until the number is under 10.
Sandra:
That's frustrating. I paid for a cleaning.
You:
I know. And I'm not trying to throw your vendor under the bus — most cleanings aren't ATP-guided. It's a different standard. Here's what I can do: full ATP-guided service today, $228, retest at the end, you get the before and after numbers in writing plus a CleanScore certificate. Then quarterly at $129. You'll never have a 'I thought it was clean' situation again — you'll have proof.
Sandra:
I want to see the after number before I commit to the ongoing program.
You:
Fair. Let's do the service. If it passes under 10, you decide about the program. I'm confident in the result.
ATP RESULT — After Service
4 RLU
Same surface — after ATP-guided deep clean
PASS — 89 → 4. Biofilm eliminated. CleanScore active.
You:
89 to 4. There's your proof. Want to go ahead with the quarterly?
Sandra:
Yes. Sign me up.
Outcome
Same-day close. Sandra cancels her existing vendor the following month. The data converted a satisfied client of a competitor into a quarterly Pinellas Ice Co account. The 89 → 4 comparison was the entire close.
Key Lesson: "We already had it cleaned" is actually an invitation — it's the perfect setup for ATP testing. Let the swab make the comparison. Don't argue about the competitor's quality; just show the number and let Sandra reach her own conclusion. Offer to do the service before asking for the program commitment — it removes the last objection.

Schedule It

Victor — quick question: do you know your ice machine's current contamination reading?

I don't even know what that means, and this is our lunch rush. Not a good time.
You:
Completely fair — I wouldn't ask you to pull the machine now. We do ice machine ATP testing and cleaning. Takes about 45 minutes, and you want it done before open or after close. When do you open tomorrow?
Victor:
11am.
You:
I can be here at 9. Free ATP test first — takes two minutes, shows you exactly where your machine stands. If it passes, you know you're good. If it doesn't, we clean it right there and retest. You're open on time either way.
Victor:
What does the cleaning cost?
You:
$228 for the full service — ATP-guided deep clean, before and after readings, CleanScore certification, starts your quarterly program. After that it's $129 a month. But let's start with the test tomorrow. If your machine is clean, you're done in two minutes for free. Deal?
Victor:
Yeah, come by at 9. I'll be here.
Saturday, 9:02am — Victor opens the door:
You:
Morning. Two minutes on the swab and we'll know.
ATP RESULT — Victor's Machine
174 RLU
Ice chute contact surface — before service
FAIL — 174 RLU. Active biofilm. Service needed before open.
You:
174 — that's a clear fail. I can have this done and retested by 9:45. You'll be fully clean and certified before your first customer walks in.
Victor:
Let's do it.
ATP RESULT — After Service
8 RLU
Same surface — 9:44am, before open
PASS — 174 → 8. Machine certified clean. Open on time.
Outcome
Close at $228 on the Saturday morning appointment. Victor enrolls in the quarterly program. The timing objection became the close — by scheduling around his operation, you became the vendor who works on his schedule, not yours.
Key Lesson: Timing objections are logistical, not emotional. Remove the logistics barrier: name a specific time that works for their operation, show up exactly on time, and be done before it matters. The Friday brush-off becomes a Saturday morning close when you make scheduling easy.

Warm Follow-Up
They Took the Card, Didn't Call
Re-Engage

ATP RESULT — Pete's Machine
117 RLU
Ice chute — current reading
FAIL — 117 RLU. Above high-risk threshold. Active citation exposure.

117 — that's in the high-risk range. Anything over 100 and an inspector will pull the machine from service. The full ATP service and quarterly program is $228 today to start.
Pete:
$228 is a lot. I didn't budget for this.
You:
I hear you. Let me put that number in context. A health citation in Pinellas County runs $250 to $500 minimum — and that's if they don't require a re-inspection visit, which adds another fee. A single shutdown during a lunch rush costs you more than $228 in lost sales in the first hour. What you're buying today is insurance against that — documented proof that your machine was tested and certified clean.
Pete:
What if I just do the cleaning once and skip the monthly thing?
You:
The $228 covers the initial service and your first month of the quarterly program — that's bundled together. Here's why that matters to you: your CleanScore sticker is only active while you're enrolled. The sticker is what shows an inspector you're on a documented maintenance program. Without the quarterly enrollment, you get a clean machine today but no ongoing certification. The sticker alone is worth it — it signals compliance before an inspector ever opens the machine.
Pete:
And after the first month?
You:
$129 a month, service every 90 days, ATP-verified. For a single machine that's $1,548 a year. That's less than most restaurants lose in one bad inspection cycle — between the citation fine, the re-inspection fee, and the reputational hit if it gets around. Think of it as a compliance subscription. You cancel anytime.
Pete:
Fine. Do the service. But if I want to cancel after the first few months?
You:
Month-to-month, no contract. You can stop anytime. I'd just ask you to give the quarterly program a full 90 days — see how your numbers look on the retest. If you're not seeing value, cancel. But most clients don't — because they've seen what a dirty machine looks like, and they don't want to go back to that.
Pete:
All right. Let's go.

5 RLU
After ATP-guided deep clean

Outcome
Close at $228. Pete stays enrolled 14 months and counting. The price objection dissolved when you reframed $228 against the cost of a single citation. Never discount — reframe the comparison.
Key Lesson: Never discount. Reframe. The question isn't "is $228 a lot?" — it's "$228 compared to what?" A citation, a shutdown, a bad Google review. The CleanScore sticker argument is underused: it's the visible proof of ongoing compliance, not just a clean machine on one day. The no-contract offer removes the last psychological barrier.
